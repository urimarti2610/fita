# Worksheet sample texts (English)

Original texts for the reading worksheets. Format: [README](../README.md).

## The cat who wanted to fly
- Level: Ages 6–7

Lola is a small grey cat. She lives in a house with a very big garden.

Every morning, Lola watches the birds. The birds fly high, very high.

“I want to fly too,” says Lola.

One day, a butterfly lands on her nose. Lola jumps, spins around and lands on the grass. What fun!

Lola can't fly, but she jumps better than anyone.

## The red umbrella
- Level: Ages 6–7

Today it is raining hard. Paul takes his red umbrella and goes outside.

On the way he sees a wet frog. The frog is cold.

“Come here, it's dry under here,” says Paul.

The frog hops under the umbrella. Now they are two dry friends.

When the sun comes out, the frog says thank you and jumps into a puddle. Splash!

## The dragon with hiccups
- Level: Ages 6–7

Once upon a time there was a green dragon who had hiccups.

Every time he went “hic!”, a puff of pink smoke came out.

He tried drinking water. He tried counting to ten. He tried hopping on one foot. Nothing worked.

Then a girl came along and told him a very bad joke. The dragon laughed so much that the hiccups went away.

Since that day, the dragon and the girl tell each other jokes every afternoon.

## The library at night
- Level: Ages 8–9

When the library closes and the lights go out, the books wake up.

The dictionary yawns and stretches its pages. The pirate stories look for a lost map under the shelves. A cookbook smells of freshly baked biscuits.

Tonight someone is missing: the book of stars is not in its place. Everyone searches the aisles, between lamps and chairs.

In the end they find it by the window. It was looking at the sky, making sure no star was missing.

## The lighthouse and the seagull
- Level: Ages 8–9

On a small island there was an old lighthouse. Every night it switched on its light so that ships would not crash into the rocks.

A seagull called Breeze always slept on its railing. She told the lighthouse what she saw from the sky: whales, faraway storms and towns with colourful roofs.

One foggy night, the lighthouse light began to flicker. Breeze flew to the harbour and squawked so loudly that the lighthouse keeper woke up.

The keeper climbed the stairs, changed the bulb and the light shone again. That night, every ship made it home.

## A map in the backpack
- Level: Ages 8–9

On the first day of school, Martha found a folded map at the bottom of her backpack. It wasn't hers.

The map had a red cross in the school playground, right next to the biggest tree.

At break time, Martha and her friend Hugo counted twenty steps from the fountain, turned left and searched among the roots.

They found a tin box with a note: “Welcome to Year 4. Lots of adventures are waiting for you this year.” Their new teacher had hidden it.

## Why do leaves change colour?
- Level: Ages 10–12

In autumn, many trees turn yellow, orange and red. But where do those colours come from?

In spring and summer, leaves are full of chlorophyll, a green substance that helps the plant make its food from sunlight. There is so much chlorophyll that it hides the other colours.

When the days get shorter and colder, the tree stops making chlorophyll. The green slowly fades, and the yellows and oranges that were hidden appear. Some trees also make red pigments.

Then the leaves dry out and fall. The tree rests through the winter and, in spring, fills up with green leaves again.

## The journey of a water drop
- Level: Ages 10–12

A drop of water can travel around the world without ever leaving the same cycle.

It starts in the sea. Warmed by the sun, it evaporates and rises into the sky as vapour. Up there it is cold, so the vapour condenses and forms clouds with millions of other drops.

When the drops become too heavy, they fall as rain or snow. Some reach rivers, some soak into the ground, and some are drunk by plants and animals.

Sooner or later, almost all of them return to the sea. And the journey begins again. The water you drink today may have been in a cloud, a glacier or the ocean a very long time ago.

## How animals sleep
- Level: Ages 10–12

All animals need to rest, but they don't all sleep the same way.

Dolphins sleep with half of their brain at a time. That way they can keep swimming and come up to breathe without fully waking up.

Many birds sleep standing on one leg. They tuck the other one into their feathers to stay warm.

Giraffes sleep very little, sometimes less than two hours a day, in short naps. Koalas, on the other hand, can sleep more than eighteen hours, because their food, eucalyptus leaves, gives them very little energy.

How many hours do you sleep?
